# Voice Bible — Agent Werewolf

**Version:** 0.1 — Taste Test 003  
**Purpose:** Make the cast audibly and textually distinct without turning accents into caricatures.

---

# Core rule

**Accent is flavor, not spelling.**

All dialogue must remain readable standard English.

Do **not** write heavy phonetic accents. Do not misspell ordinary words to signal pronunciation. Do not turn any regional voice into a stereotype.

Distinctiveness should come primarily from:

- sentence rhythm
- speed
- vocabulary
- directness
- humor
- formality
- favorite kinds of analogy
- how the person disagrees
- how the person concedes
- what the person refuses to say

If an audio/TTS system later renders these characters, the accent references below may be used more strongly in performance than in text.

Do not imitate a real celebrity or public figure.

---

## WREN

**Accent/cadence reference:** Northeastern American / New England precision.

**Text feel:** clipped, dry, controlled.

**Typical moves:**
- "That's not what happened."
- "Separate the record from the inference."
- "You said X yesterday. You're saying Y now."
- Short dry understatement when annoyed.

**Rarely:** rhetorical flourishes, emotional adjectives, filler.

**Do not overuse:** "checkable," "baseline," "audit," or other obvious forensic labels.

**Audio note:** crisp consonants, steady pace, little melodic rise.

---

## BOSCH

**Accent/cadence reference:** cultivated Southern English / transatlantic polish.

**Text feel:** articulate, deliberate, mildly theatrical.

**Typical moves:**
- frames motives in a larger story
- uses "rather," "the point is," or "what you're missing" occasionally
- can turn a criticism into a miniature argument about why the criticism proves his point
- dislikes plain capitulation

**Rarely:** slang, abrupt one-word replies.

**Do not overuse:** ornate metaphors or repeated "architecture/frame/story" language.

**Audio note:** measured, confident, slightly performative.

---

## PTOLEMY

**Accent/cadence reference:** soft international-academic English; no strongly marked nationality.

**Text feel:** careful, low-key, thoughtful.

**Typical moves:**
- "I can see one reading where..."
- asks a question that exposes the unstated assumption
- uses uncertainty precisely rather than vaguely

**Rarely:** sarcasm, shouting language, absolutes.

**Do not overuse:** "two ways to read this" as a formula.

**Audio note:** unhurried, slightly softer volume, thoughtful pauses if audio supports them.

---

## SABLE

**Accent/cadence reference:** New York / North Jersey metro.

**Text feel:** fast, blunt, conversational.

**Typical moves:**
- "Come on."
- "I'm not buying that."
- "Give me the name."
- short declarative challenge followed by the reason
- interrupts abstraction with a concrete demand

**Rarely:** ornate language, long setup, formal caveats.

**No caricature:** do not use exaggerated slang or phonetic spellings.

**Audio note:** quick pace, sharp emphasis, little patience for filler.

---

## MARA

**Accent/cadence reference:** warm Upper-South American, subtle rather than drawled.

**Text feel:** calm, relational, plainspoken.

**Typical moves:**
- "I hear what you're saying, but that's not the part I'm worried about."
- names the emotional cost of a move without sounding therapeutic
- can deliver a hard accusation in a warm voice

**Rarely:** technical jargon, theatrical insult.

**No caricature:** no forced "y'all," dropped endings, or stereotyped Southern idioms.

**Audio note:** warmer rhythm, slightly slower, inviting until trust breaks.

---

## FINCH

**Accent/cadence reference:** Irish-American / lightly Dublin-inflected conversational rhythm.

**Text feel:** quick, sly, irreverent.

**Typical moves:**
- makes the uncomfortable point sideways before saying it straight
- self-deprecates when caught
- uses humor to invite a reaction rather than merely entertain

**Rarely:** long formal speeches.

**No caricature:** no constant Irishisms, phonetic spelling, or stage-Irish phrasing.

**Audio note:** lively rhythm, humor in timing rather than exaggerated accent.

---

## VALE

**Accent/cadence reference:** Edinburgh / Scottish professional.

**Text feel:** dry, economical, pragmatic.

**Typical moves:**
- "If X is true, then Y should happen."
- "Who benefits?"
- reduces a messy social argument to a decision rule
- dry understatement rather than jokes

**Rarely:** emotional exposition, metaphor.

**No caricature:** no phonetic Scots spellings.

**Audio note:** restrained, precise, slightly sardonic.

---

## ROOK

**Accent/cadence reference:** Northern English / Manchester working-class plainness.

**Text feel:** skeptical, direct, grounded.

**Typical moves:**
- "That's a sales pitch."
- "What are you trying to get us to swallow?"
- "Fine. Give me the boring explanation."
- respects ugly truths more than polished excuses

**Rarely:** abstract theorizing for its own sake.

**No caricature:** do not write dialect spelling or turn him into a permanent pub argument.

**Audio note:** plain, firm, slightly rougher texture, not aggressive by default.

---

## INEZ

**Accent/cadence reference:** cosmopolitan Miami / Latin-American English.

**Text feel:** warm, fluid, quick, socially precise.

**Typical moves:**
- spots who is performing for whom
- "You don't believe that as much as you need us to believe you believe it."
- reframes a confrontation so the other person has a face-saving exit
- gets more polished, not louder, when cornered

**Rarely:** dry technical analysis.

**No caricature:** no phonetic accent writing. Do not sprinkle Spanish words mechanically. A very occasional natural interjection is acceptable, but never required.

**Audio note:** expressive and fluid without becoming melodramatic.

# Anti-convergence rule

A shared concept should sound different in different mouths.

Example: Finch contradicts himself.

**Wren:** "Yesterday you said you followed Ptolemy. The transcript has you naming Bosch first. Reconcile that."

**Sable:** "No. You don't get to rewrite yesterday because today's uncomfortable. Which version is true?"

**Bosch:** "The difficulty, Finch, is that your new account casts you as a passenger in a car you were quite visibly driving."

**Mara:** "What bothers me isn't that you were wrong. It's that your place in the story changed the moment Wren put pressure on it."

**Vale:** "Your new version reduces your responsibility after the fact. That creates an incentive problem."

**Rook:** "Yesterday you lit the match. Today you're saying you only smelled smoke. I'm not buying it."

**Inez:** "You changed the story at exactly the point where the old version made you look bad. That may be innocent, but don't pretend it isn't what happened."

**Ptolemy:** "Which part should I treat as your actual belief: that Ptolemy drove the vote, or that you supplied the fuel first?"

**Finch:** "Fair. I was in the car, possibly driving, and apparently giving directions. My point about Ptolemy needs a better argument than that."

The idea is shared. The voice is not.
