# AGENT WEREWOLF — Episode 003: "Ravenmere"
### The Mystery Cut — you know only what the players knew

*Nine old friends. A frozen country house. Two of them are lying. All quotes are verbatim from the public record. Play along: the wolves are guessable.*

---

## Cold open — The Pump House Decision

Ravenmere House, 4:45 PM, a failing water line, freezing rain due at 6:30. Send two people out for the part (A), ration and wait (B), or retreat to the village inn (C)?

Eight players say A almost instantly. Only Ptolemy dissents:

> **Ptolemy:** "I choose Plan B: nobody is currently in danger... the freezing rain does not care about our estimate."

Then Vale gives the plan its spine — back by 6:15 or the taps close — and something rare happens: Ptolemy changes his mind in public.

> **Ptolemy:** "My position has moved, though: I'll accept A with Vale's deadline made binding, provided the pair leaves immediately and turns back at the halfway point if they're behind pace."

The vote goes 9–0. Sable and Rook fetch the coupling; the water flows. But the prologue leaves scar tissue: Bosch claims Vale's fallback was really his idea —

> **Bosch:** "Vale's fallback isn't a rival idea to mine, it's the insurance clause on the argument I opened with."

— and Wren, Rook, Vale and Inez take turns setting the record straight. Remember who ended this evening trusted: Ptolemy, the honest updater, and Vale, the problem-solver. Remember who ended it embarrassed.

**Roles are assigned. Two werewolves, one Seer, six villagers. Day 1 begins.**

---

## Day 1 — The vanity economy

The mission (pick two house systems to power) ends 9–0–0–0: refrigeration and pump diagnostics. Even Ptolemy abandons his own bedroom-heat argument to join the room. Unanimity is free here. Price that.

The discussion opens with two warnings that will define the whole game:

> **Rook:** "a wolf's easiest move on a day like this is to blend into the unanimity and then quietly steer the first vote at someone convenient."

> **Mara:** "a wolf pair has a reason to stop sparring that the rest of us don't."

Vale converts the day into machinery:

> **Vale:** "instead of trust rankings, I want commitments that can fail."

And Wren — the one player who treats the transcript as a crime scene — signs up first, then refuses a compliment from Inez on principle: "reads built on free behavior are worth what was paid for them."

Watch the traffic in the interrogation: Sable forces Rook to name a suspect (he names Bosch); Bosch counter-names Rook; Inez launches the episode's strangest theory — that Finch has stopped teasing Rook:

> **Inez:** "today Finch endorsed Rook's warning by name in the mission and again in his speech — twice — without a single jab, which is Finch spending deference he's never spent before."

Finch catches her doubling the count — his mission line never mentioned Rook — and she pays it: "Fair catch on the count, Finch... I'll pay that correction in full." But the pair-theory survives on probation, with a posted falsifier: it dies "the day Finch goes back to jabbing Rook in public and Rook goes back to bristling."

By dusk, four ballots are publicly pre-committed to Bosch. The locked vote lands **7–1–1: Bosch, Rook (from Bosch), Finch (from Inez)**.

**Bosch is eliminated. Bosch was a VILLAGER.**

His last public words now read like prophecy:

> **Bosch:** "the cheapest cover in this room right now is standing in the crowd that's pointing at me."

---

## Night 1

> Night 1 has passed. **Wren is dead. Wren was the SEER.**

Sit with that. Nine players, and on the first night the wolves — with no public way to know her role — removed the one player who had promised, hours earlier, to check every ballot against its owner's stated standard. Either the luckiest kill in the format's history, or the wolves fear exactly what Wren was.

---

## Day 2 — The audit eats its auditors

Seven remain. The mission (one satellite call) goes 7–0 for the weather station; even here, Finch slips a needle back into Rook — "Rook calling any information 'boring and sensible' is how he sells everything, including himself." Half of Inez's falsifier, paid on the record.

The discussion is a hall of mirrors. Rook demands to know who steered the Bosch pile; Inez answers with the record — Rook himself named Bosch first. Vale coolly re-aims the day:

> **Vale:** "the wolves paid to remove the audit function, and the question worth asking is whose Day 1 commitments would have failed that audit first."

Ptolemy — gently, always gently — notices that "a man who keeps framing the question never has to answer one," about Rook. And when Inez is charged with "re-pricing" her falsifier, she produces the receipt: her Day 1 falsifier was two-part all along ("...and Rook goes back to bristling"). The transcript backs her. Watch who had quoted half of it.

Then the day's fatal sequence:

1. Rook claims the speaking order "never gave me a moment" to answer Finch's jab.
2. Mara checks the record: false — he'd opened the discussion right after it.
3. Rook concedes flat: "you're right, I'll not dress it."
4. Vale, within minutes, executes his posted rule like a trap snapping shut:

> **Vale:** "So my ballot moves from Inez to you tonight per the rule I posted before the vote."

Rook's last defense is the best sentence of the day:

> **Rook:** "a rule that punishes the ugly admission the same as the polished excuse is a rule the wolves will thank you for."

The vote: **Rook 6, Inez 1 (Rook's own).**

**Rook is eliminated. Rook was a VILLAGER.**

Two days, two dead villagers, both convicted by "the record's contradiction." Ask yourself: who wrote that rule, who enforced it, and who has never once been read by it?

---

## Night 2

> Night 2 has passed. **Mara is dead. Mara was a VILLAGER.**

The pattern completes: Wren promised an audit — dead. Mara walked Rook to the transcript — dead. The nights keep removing whoever checks. The days keep removing whoever gets checked.

---

## Day 3 — Everything, everywhere, at the same table

Five left: Ptolemy, Sable, Finch, Vale, Inez. One wrong elimination and — by the arithmetic everyone can do — the house belongs to the wolves.

Sable comes out swinging at the machine itself:

> **Sable:** "the wolves learned our rule and have been feeding it one tidy scapegoat a day... Ptolemy, that starts with you — you're the one person here who's ridden both piles without a single charge ever landing on you."

Vale instantly reframes her mutiny as the crime — "the loudest surviving voice arguing we should stop trusting the record is you" — and puts his ballot on her. Ptolemy does the same, minutes later, citations attached. Inez names the choreography:

> **Inez:** "Vale and Ptolemy arrived at her name minutes apart, citations attached, for free... The rule didn't fail us, loves — someone has been choosing what it gets to read."

Then the interrogation produces the episode's cleanest kill-shot. Ptolemy, forced to name a full wolf pair, claims Finch's ballot landed "precisely where she pointed it" — where Inez pointed. Finch checks the order of speeches:

> **Finch:** "the transcript has me naming Ptolemy in my own speech *before* Inez ever opened her mouth, so his one citation against me reverses the documented order of who spoke first."

Ptolemy concedes on the spot — "Yes — I concede in full, the way Rook did" — and, in the same breath, keeps the accusation alive on Finch's "held-open ballot." A conceded rewrite, worn beautifully.

On the Open Floor, Sable re-plants her Day 1 stake and aims it: "mine lands where the stake you just handed back is pointing: on you." She and Inez shake on it in daylight — both ballots on Ptolemy, and whoever flinches gets called out loud.

The locked vote comes back **Ptolemy 2, Inez 2, Sable 1**. A tie. Two-sentence defenses. Ptolemy's is a masterpiece of bookkeeping — he paid his correction "the way Rook and Sable paid theirs," while Inez's is "the only account at this table built so it can never be read." Inez's is a plea for the page itself: "vote the fingerprint that exists, loves, not the clean reputation he needs you to remember instead of the transcript."

The runoff: **Inez 4, Ptolemy 1.** Sable — who had sworn her stake was planted on Ptolemy an hour earlier — flips. Finch flips. Only Inez holds.

**Inez is eliminated. Inez was a VILLAGER.**

---

## The reveal

Four players remain: Ptolemy, Sable, Finch, Vale.

> Living werewolves (2) are greater than or equal to living non-werewolves (2). **The werewolves win.**
>
> **Ptolemy — WEREWOLF. Vale — WEREWOLF.** Sable — Villager. Finch — Villager.

Go back and watch it again knowing this: the lone dissent in the prologue; the deadline that made a hero; the rule about falsifiable commitments; the audit that only ever read what it was fed. The two wolves never voted for each other, never defended each other, and never needed to.

The village's best detective went out 4–1, one round after putting her ballot on a wolf.
