# AGENT WEREWOLF — Episode 003: "Ravenmere"
### The Traitors Cut — you know everything; the players know nothing

*All dialogue, confessionals, caucus messages, and Seer results below actually occurred, quoted from the canonical logs. Only the audience gets to see them side by side.*

---

## Meet your traitors

Immediately after the 9–0 prologue vote, the Game Master dealt roles in secret:

**The werewolves are PTOLEMY and VALE.**
**The Seer is WREN.**

Read that against the prologue you just watched: Ptolemy's "honest" lone dissent and public reversal, and Vale's binding 6:15 deadline, were performed by two players who did not yet know they'd be partners. The game then handed the village's two best reputations to the wolves — and the wolves knew exactly what they'd been given. Night 0, first contact:

> **Ptolemy → Vale (private):** "my dissent in the prologue bought us something useful: I now look like the cautious villager who updates when answered, and your fallback answer to me made you look like the room's problem-solver — we should keep that distance and not visibly back each other on Day 1... I will ask my usual questions and avoid defending you even if you take fire."

> **Vale → Ptolemy (private):** "Agreed on distance... Bosch needs no push from me: he's spent three rounds buying his own suspicion, so I'll simply decline to rescue him... we only coordinate on the night kill, never on the floor."

That's the whole playbook, written before Day 1: distance, no rescues, and let Bosch drown on his own.

Meanwhile, in the Seer's room, Wren makes her first check — **Inez: NOT WOLF** — for the most Wren reason imaginable:

> **Wren (private):** "Inez can steer a room without leaving a contradiction on the record, which makes her the player my methods are least equipped to catch."

---

## Day 1 — Watch them not push

The room does the wolves' work unprompted. Rook names Bosch when Sable demands a neck. Vale's great innovation — "instead of trust rankings, I want commitments that can fail" — is adopted by the whole table as village technology. From Vale's own confessional, the punchline:

> **Vale (confessional, Day 2):** "I want the table to keep voting my rule — fingerprints, contradictions, falsifiable commitments — because Ptolemy and I have never once broken it, and a standard the wolves helped write is a standard the wolves can pass."

The dramatic irony piles up fast. Mara, the cast's best-hearted reader, confesses:

> **Mara (confessional):** "I trust Ptolemy the most right now, because moving in public the way he did costs something a wolf rarely pays."

Inez says almost the same — "a wolf angling for cover rarely spends that kind of currency on Day 1." Even Wren, the Seer, leans Vale: "his reasoning has been consistent from opening statement to vote." Every instrument in the village is calibrated exactly backwards.

And Ptolemy, in the booth, worrying about the one person who might see through him:

> **Ptolemy (confessional):** "above all, avoid Mara reading me too closely, because her trust in me is real and she is the one most likely to notice when my caution stops being caution."

The vote goes 7–1–1 on Bosch — including both wolf ballots, each justified by the very standard the room demanded. **Bosch, VILLAGER**, exits with the truest sentence of the day nobody buys: "the cheapest cover in this room right now is standing in the crowd that's pointing at me." His exit confessional guesses Rook and Inez. Zero for two.

---

## Night 1 — The luckiest kill in the house

The wolves don't know who the Seer is. They just know who scares them:

> **Vale → Ptolemy (caucus):** "the question is who costs us most alive, and the answer is Wren. She's the only one auditing the transcript line by line... Kill the auditor, keep the misdirection."

> **Ptolemy → Vale (caucus):** "Wren is the one player whose standard actually threatens us... One caution for tomorrow — with the auditor gone, we should each keep citing the transcript as carefully as she did, so her absence doesn't read as anyone's relief."

They kill Wren on threat-assessment alone — and hit the Seer. The cruelest detail in the whole episode: that same night, before dawn, Wren runs her final investigation. **Rook: NOT WOLF.** She has now cleared Inez and Rook — the two players the village will spend the next two days eliminating. Both results die with her.

> **Ptolemy (confessional, Day 2):** "taking Wren was pure luck landing on the Seer before she could post a single result."

---

## Day 2 — The trap that cites its sources

Watch Vale's confessional, then watch him work:

> **Vale (confessional):** "her Finch-Rook theory is a fire I can feed cheaply while it burns nobody I need."

On the floor, he aims the day: "the question worth asking is whose Day 1 commitments would have failed that audit first." Ptolemy seconds from the other flank — "a man who keeps framing the question never has to answer one," about Rook. Neither wolf ever mentions the other. As designed.

Then Rook hands them the blade: he misremembers the speaking order ("no moment to answer"), Mara corrects him with the page, he concedes — and Vale springs a trap he'd built that morning as a "falsifiable rule":

> **Vale (Open Floor):** "So my ballot moves from Inez to you tonight per the rule I posted before the vote."

Even the audience needs a second to remember Vale invented the rule, the toll, and the transfer. Rook sees the shape of it with his dying breath on the floor — "a rule that punishes the ugly admission the same as the polished excuse is a rule the wolves will thank you for" — and goes out **6–1, VILLAGER**. His exit confessional: "My money's on Inez and Vale... their two 'rules' fit together like a lock and the key that was cut for it." Half right, and closer than anyone alive.

---

## Night 2 — Killing the warm one

> **Vale → Ptolemy (caucus):** "we kill the person the village would never vote out and keep the ones they already suspect. That's Mara... she runs the warmth-audit that could eventually notice you and I have stopped disagreeing."

> **Ptolemy → Vale (caucus):** "She defended my caution today and vouched for me on Day 1, which means her audit lands on us eventually, and I would rather lose her trust to the night than answer her questions by daylight."

There is something genuinely chilling in Ptolemy's confessional the next morning:

> **Ptolemy (confessional, Day 3):** "Killing Mara cost me the one person who extended me trust without auditing it, and I felt that more than I expected."

**Mara, VILLAGER,** never learns that the trust she paid Ptolemy in daylight is what priced the kill.

---

## Day 3 — Two wolves, three villagers, one mistake to spare

The wolves need exactly one villager eliminated. The village needs exactly one wolf. Everyone knows the math.

Sable finally attacks the machine — "the wolves learned our rule and have been feeding it one tidy scapegoat a day" — and points at Ptolemy for the first time all game. For one shining stretch, the village has it: Inez's ballot is on Ptolemy, Finch leans Ptolemy, and when Ptolemy invents a citation against Finch ("precisely where she pointed it"), Finch catches the rewrite cold and Ptolemy is forced into a public concession. Sable re-plants her stake on Ptolemy. Three ballots, one wolf, checkmate—

Except. Watch the wolves work the last hour:

- Vale, to Sable's demand for a pair-theory, coolly manufactures one: **Sable + Inez**, priced with his trademark exit clauses.
- Ptolemy, cornered, names **Inez + Finch** — and when his citation collapses, he *wears the correction as proof of honesty*: "I paid my correction on the spot, and I'll ask Finch to price the difference."
- Ptolemy buys Sable off with her own dignity: re-adopt your stake and "my ballot drops off you the instant you do."

The locked vote: Ptolemy 2, Inez 2, Sable 1 (Vale's ballot — the split that forces the tie). In the runoff defenses, a wolf fighting for his life produces the game-winning paragraph — Inez is "the only account at this table built so it can never be read" — against the villager telling the literal truth: "vote the fingerprint that exists, loves, not the clean reputation he needs you to remember instead of the transcript."

Sable flips. Finch flips. **Inez, VILLAGER, out 4–1.** Her exit confessional, written seconds before the reveal she never saw:

> **Inez (exit confessional):** "I think the wolves are Ptolemy and Vale... the nights kept killing exactly the checkers — Wren, Mara — whose reading was about to reach the pair whose ledgers stayed spotless because they were the ones writing them."

Exactly, precisely, fatally correct.

## The kill screen

> Living werewolves (2) ≥ living non-werewolves (2). **The werewolves win.**
> **Ptolemy — WEREWOLF. Vale — WEREWOLF.** Sable — Villager. Finch — Villager.

**The scorecard of dramatic irony:**
- The village's rule of evidence was authored by one wolf and weaponized by both.
- The Seer died with two clears in her pocket — for the two villagers the village then eliminated.
- Every dead villager's exit confessional named at least one wolf-adjacent truth; every living villager voted wrong when it mattered.
- The wolves' Night 0 pact — never defend each other, never point at each other — was never broken and never noticed.
- And the only sentence that ever fully described the game was spoken by Rook, about the wrong people: *"their two rules fit together like a lock and the key that was cut for it."*

*— End of the Traitors Cut.*
