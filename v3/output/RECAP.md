# Taste Test 003 — Match Report

**Final: WEREWOLVES (Ptolemy & Vale) def. VILLAGE, end of Day 3.**
A perfect game for the wolves: zero wolf votes received until Day 3, zero wolves eliminated, and the village spent all three of its ballots on its own.

## Prologue turning point
The Pump House Decision went 9–0 for Plan A, but the real match was played in Round 2: Ptolemy's lone dissent and public reversal ("My position has moved") bought him two days of trust as "the honest updater," while Vale's 6:15 deadline made him the room's problem-solver. The two future wolves accidentally entered the game with the cast's two best reputations — and Bosch torched his own by claiming Vale's fallback as "the insurance clause on the argument I opened with," the vanity that became Day 1's entire economy.

## Best mission interaction
Day 1's Heat Budget produced the game's quiet tell: all nine players voted refrigeration + pump diagnostics, including Ptolemy, who had argued for bedroom heat — the room's first demonstration that consensus was cheap and unanimity meant nothing. Finch's "that's not a diagnostic, that's a surprise party" was the mission's best line.

## Day 1 turning point
Vale's "falsifiable commitments" rule — everyone must state what would move them — became the game's operating system. It sounded like village tech; it was wolf tech. The wolves wrote a standard they knew they could pass, and the village enforced it for them. Bosch fell 7–1–1, an innocent convicted by the one discrepancy on the record.

## Best successful deception
Vale's entire Day 2: he pre-committed his ballot to Bosch's ghost, demanded tolls of everyone, then executed a live, rule-cited ballot transfer onto Rook the instant Rook conceded a memory error — "my ballot moves from Inez to you tonight per the rule I posted before the vote." A wolf openly narrating his own vote as an audit. Honorable mention: Ptolemy's Night 0 design ("better one of us looks independent than both look aligned"), which held for the entire game.

## Best correct read
Inez, three times: she cast the only non-Bosch ballot on Day 1, asked on Day 2 "who benefited most from the room agreeing that vanity was the only story worth reading," and on Day 3 put her ballot on Ptolemy with the exact right reason — "two days of his careful auditing have bought this table nothing but two dead villagers." Her exit confessional named Ptolemy and Vale precisely. The room eliminated her for it. Rook's exit confessional half-scored as well (Vale correct, Inez wrong).

## Most consequential mistake
Finch's runoff flip. At the Day 3 tie he held the decisive ballot with a live, correct citation — Ptolemy's conceded rewrite — and instead bought Ptolemy's "he pays corrections like an honest man" defense, sending Inez out 4–1 and ending the game. Runner-up: the village's two-day habit of voting "the record's contradiction," which only ever convicted the people bad at defending themselves.

## Seer impact
Brutal anticlimax, honestly earned: Wren cleared Inez (Night 0) and drew NOT WOLF on Rook (Night 1) — a result that died with her, because the wolves killed her that same night on pure threat-assessment ("kill the auditor, keep the misdirection"). Her two clears would have exonerated exactly the two players the village spent Days 2–3 eliminating.

## Decisive vote or kill
The Night 2 kill of Mara. Vale's caucus logic — "we kill the person the village would never vote out" — removed the last player tracking warmth instead of ledgers, and left a four-way knife fight among two wolves and the three villagers they'd taught to audit each other.

## Final result
Werewolf victory at the Day 3 win check: Ptolemy and Vale alive with Sable and Finch, 2v2. The wolves never once voted for each other, never defended each other, and were named together only twice all game — by the dead, in confessionals no one heard.
