# Match Report — Game 03

*Contains the reveal. Read the blind transcript first.*

## Full time: a 1–1–1–1 split, the wolf walks, and the only player who called it from the opening whistle spent three rounds being ignored for it.

**The opening accusation — or rather, the opening confession.** Ptolemy drew first speech and did something remarkable: he described, in mild and unhurried tones, exactly what a werewolf's first move would look like — "sound reasonable without actually committing to anything" — and then executed that move to the letter, closing with a polite question to Wren. Wren, speaking second, caught it instantly: "a deflection wrapped in a question." Sable, speaking last, went further: "that's not cleverness, that's a confession." Round one, and two players were staring directly at the answer.

**The first reversal.** It never came from the two who needed one. Instead, Bosch — third to speak — looked at the board, ignored the self-incriminating monologue sitting in plain view, and built his cathedral on Wren: she'd inverted Ptolemy's framing, she'd offered no reading of herself, she was "the wolf who read the room in an instant." From that moment the match stopped being a hunt and became a duel. Wren spent rounds two and three prosecuting Bosch's "preparation"; Bosch spent them expanding the Wren canvas — "my brushes are down." Neither ever looked back at the quiet man asking all the questions.

**The key contradiction.** Sable, round two: "Watching someone describe a werewolf's exact move and then execute it himself." That is, on the record, the single most accurate sentence spoken in this match. And it bought her nothing — because it came from Sable, who commits in round one on gut and never budges, and whose certainty the table had already priced in as noise. Ptolemy even converted her rigidity into his own defense, wondering aloud whether certainty that rigid "tends to protect itself." The wolf used his accuser's reputation as his shield.

**The final vote.** Wren: Bosch — "a managed story delivered by someone who already knew the ending." Bosch: Wren — the canvas, richer than ever. Ptolemy: Sable, uncertainty duly stated, question duly appended. Sable: Ptolemy — "I've never needed another reason." Four players, four different names. No conviction. Wolf walks.

**The reveal.** The wolf was **Ptolemy**. He survived not by being invisible but by being *plausibly* suspicious — suspicious in exactly the way innocent Ptolemy always looks, so the one player who nailed him couldn't be told apart from the player who nails somebody every game. Sable was right from the opening breath. Nobody believed her. Her scouting report warned this exact tragedy was coming: *burns credibility being loudly wrong — when she is finally right, nobody believes her.* The Wren–Bosch war did the rest, splitting the vote four ways and holding the door open as the wolf strolled out.

**Play of the match:** Ptolemy's opening speech — narrating the wolf's playbook aloud, as the wolf, and getting away with it in a hall full of detectives.
