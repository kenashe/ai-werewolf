# Match Report — Game 01

*Contains the reveal. Read the blind transcript first.*

## Full time: village 3, wolf 1. The loudest man in the room was the wolf, and he never stopped talking long enough to notice everyone had noticed.

**The opening accusation.** Sable came off the line the way Sable always comes off the line — at full speed, at the quietest target. "Hasn't said a word yet and that silence is doing a lot of work," she said of Ptolemy, before Ptolemy had been given a chance to do any work at all. Textbook Sable: pick a lane in the first ten seconds, drive in it.

**Then Bosch stood up.** Speaking second, with nothing on the board but Sable's opening salvo, Bosch delivered a fully furnished prosecution of Wren — motive, mechanism, and a flourish about "opportunism and complicity" being cousins. It was a beautiful piece of architecture. It was also, we now know, the wolf's opening move: name someone early, name them elaborately, and hope the table spends three rounds arguing about your theory instead of your role.

**The first reversal — and the turning point.** Round two, first words out of Sable's mouth: "Still Ptolemy. Wait — actually—" and just like that, the game's most stubborn player un-stubborned. She swung her gun from Ptolemy to Bosch mid-sentence, on pure texture: "that locked-in certainty before anyone spoke is a tell, not confidence." Bosch, fatally, treated the pivot as a gift. "How delicious — Sable has pivoted, which tells me everything I need to know." He expanded the theory to absorb her: Sable was now Wren's panicked ally. The theory was getting bigger. The room was getting smaller.

**The key contradiction.** Ptolemy, of all people, landed the cleanest blow of the match without ever making an accusation: "Bosch just answered my question by not answering it." Three rounds, and Bosch never once named a concrete thing Wren had actually *done* — only the shape of things she might have meant. Wren filed it in her ledger and returned it with interest: "The architecture Bosch keeps invoking is wallpaper over absence."

**The final vote.** Wren: Bosch. Ptolemy: Bosch — hedged, questioning, but Bosch. Sable: Bosch, with a told-you-so already loaded. Bosch: Wren, riding his round-one canvas all the way into the sea. 3–1.

**The reveal.** The wolf was **Bosch**. The village got him — and the postgame irony writes itself: the wolf's cover story was so ornate, so lovingly maintained, and so completely unrevised that its very consistency became the evidence. He was caught not for slipping, but for never moving. Sable's mid-match gut call, the one nobody had to believe, turned out to be the game-winner.

**Play of the match:** Sable's round-two swerve — "Wait — actually" — the moment the hunt found its scent.
