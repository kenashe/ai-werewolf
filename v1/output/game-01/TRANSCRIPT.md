# Taste Test 001 — Game 01

**Players:** Wren, Bosch, Ptolemy, Sable
**Format:** Four players, one hidden werewolf, three public discussion rounds, no eliminations, final public vote.

## Round 1
*Speaking order: Sable, Bosch, Ptolemy, Wren*

**Sable:** Ptolemy. Hasn't said a word yet and that silence is doing a lot of work. I've seen that move before — play quiet, let everyone else's noise cover you. We're watching you, Ptolemy.

**Bosch:** Well, I've already formed my view, and I see no reason to rush past it — the architecture of suspicion here is quite plain to anyone paying attention. Wren has said nothing, which is precisely what a careful operator does when the opening move belongs to someone else. Notice how Sable leapt first at Ptolemy — a confident opening that conveniently draws every eye away from the quiet figure at the edge of the room. Wren benefits from that misdirection whether it was arranged or merely seized upon, and in my experience, opportunism and complicity are cousins. I name Wren, and my reasoning is already settled.

**Ptolemy:** Something feels a little rehearsed about this opening — Sable moves fast, Bosch follows with a ready-made verdict, and both of them land somewhere convenient. I'd rather watch a bit longer before I commit to any shape of it. Bosch, what specifically in Wren's behavior felt like deliberate quiet, rather than just being the last to speak?

**Wren:** No prior statement from Bosch exists to quote directly against a contradiction, so a provisional read: Bosch arrived with a verdict already sealed before the round began, which is not reasoning — it is a costume worn over a decision already made. The convenient tightness of Bosch's certainty marks him as the one to watch.

- **Ptolemy** — Suspect: Bosch | Confidence: 38 | Reason: Arriving in round one with a verdict already sealed, complete with elaborate rhetorical framing, suggests performance over genuine reasoning — though I hold that loosely, since Wren noticed it too, which could cut either way.
- **Sable** — Suspect: Bosch | Confidence: 62 | Reason: Came in with a locked verdict before anyone had said enough to build one — that's not instinct, that's a script.

## Round 2
*Speaking order: Sable, Bosch, Ptolemy, Wren*

**Sable:** Still Ptolemy. Wait — actually, Bosch came in with a verdict already gift-wrapped and I'm not ignoring that. My gut says Bosch, not Ptolemy — that locked-in certainty before anyone spoke is a tell, not confidence. Ptolemy asked the right question; Bosch didn't answer it.

**Bosch:** How delicious — Sable has pivoted, which tells me everything I need to know, because the wolf doesn't hold a position, the wolf adjusts when the wind shifts. My theory regarding Wren has not moved an inch, and I'll tell you why: Wren arrived in Round One with language so precisely aimed — "costume worn over a decision already made" — that it could only have been constructed in advance, a counter-narrative prepared for exactly the moment someone named them. Sable's sudden reversal, meanwhile, is the mist that follows a panic, and a panicked ally of Wren's does exactly what Sable just did — abandons a safe target the moment Wren needed a distraction. Ptolemy asked a pointed question but produced no theory of their own, which is the quieter cousin of Wren's gambit: let others duel while you remain untouchable. The architecture holds, and I have no interest in renovating it simply because the walls are being rattled.

**Ptolemy:** Bosch just answered my question by not answering it — more architecture, more elaborate framing, and still no specific behavior from Wren, only the shape of one. Sable's shift actually interests me more than the shift itself: it moved the moment Bosch's certainty became conspicuous, which could mean gut instinct or it could mean something else. Wren, you've been named twice now — what do you make of Bosch's reasoning, in plain terms?

**Wren:** "Bosch has not moved an inch" — and that consistency is being sold as virtue, but Ptolemy named the mechanism precisely: elaborate framing substituted for evidence, twice in sequence. The architecture Bosch keeps invoking is wallpaper over absence.

- **Sable** — Suspect: Bosch | Confidence: 78 | Reason: He doubled down with more elaborate framing instead of answering Ptolemy's direct question, and that kind of loud, unshakeable certainty covering a complete absence of actual evidence reads as a script, not instinct.

## Round 3
*Speaking order: Ptolemy, Bosch, Sable, Wren*

**Ptolemy:** Bosch has had three rounds to name a single concrete thing Wren actually did, and has spent all three constructing reasons why the lack of evidence is itself evidence — that pattern doesn't open, it closes. Sable's shift felt instinctive rather than strategic, which is a different texture from what I've been watching across the table. Sable, now that we're at the end: when you moved off Ptolemy and toward Bosch, what were you actually seeing, not feeling?

**Bosch:** The architecture does not waver simply because three rounds have passed and the pressure has found a familiar shape — in fact, pressure is the instrument that reveals structure, and what it has revealed here is Wren, consistently, without exception. Ptolemy asks for a single concrete thing, as though the texture of a move — the pre-packaged rhetorical strike, the precision of "costume worn over a decision," the follow-up "wallpaper over absence" — is somehow less than concrete because it lives in language rather than gesture. Wren came to this game with a script, and the script is visible in the seams: nobody discovers that kind of phrasing under pressure, they arrive with it, which means the pressure was anticipated, which means the naming was anticipated, which means Wren knew they would be named before the round began. Sable's shift, Ptolemy's Socratic patience, these are the weather around the fact — and the fact is Wren. My vote is already written.

**Sable:** Bosch. Three rounds and not one concrete behavior, just scaffolding dressed up as certainty — my gut called it in round two and I'm not walking it back. Ptolemy asked what I was seeing: I was seeing a man who already had his story before anyone opened their mouth, and that's not confidence, that's a mask.

**Wren:** "Bosch came to this game with a story, and the story has not moved because it cannot move — movement would require evidence, and there is none." Three rounds of architecture without a single brick: my vote is Bosch.
