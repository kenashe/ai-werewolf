# Match Report — Game 02

*Contains the reveal. Read the blind transcript first.*

## Full time: wolf escapes with a clean sheet. Zero votes. The village convicted its own most careful member instead.

**The opening accusation.** Bosch drew the first speaking slot and did what Bosch does with a first speaking slot: he built a cathedral. Ptolemy was the wolf, he declared, because patience is the wolf's virtue and Ptolemy is patient — a theory constructed before Ptolemy had said one word. Sable followed and, in a rare moment of alignment she would never admit to, also pointed at Ptolemy — while making sure to elbow Bosch on the way past: "don't think your fancy frame hides the fact that you were the first to point fingers."

**The quiet game of the eventual winner.** Go back and watch the tape on Sable. Round one: hits Ptolemy, jabs Bosch, commits to nothing that could be falsified. Round two: pivots cleanly to Wren — "steering this thing from the back." Round three: "a wolf with a clipboard," plus an order barked at Ptolemy to stop asking questions and vote. Three rounds, three targets serviced, heat never once landing on her. It looked like classic scattergun Sable. It was a wolf changing lanes every time a lane got crowded.

**The key contradiction.** Wren spent the match doing forensic accounting — "two rounds in, that position is still unexamined... not one of the three has staked a falsifiable claim" — and it was true, and it was devastating, and it was aimed at the wrong player. The record's only *consistent* pattern, she concluded, was Ptolemy's evasion-by-question. Which brings us to the flaw the scouting report warned about: Wren trusts consistency over content. Sable was never consistent. That inconsistency read to Wren as noise, not as a wolf steering.

**The pile-on.** Ptolemy's questions — the genuinely innocent kind — became the whole case against him. Bosch: "performing neutrality, which is signal." Wren: "consistency of evasion is the only signal on the table." The gentlest player at the table watched two prosecutors converge on him from opposite directions while the wolf stood behind them nodding.

**The final vote.** Wren: Ptolemy. Bosch: Ptolemy — "less surprised than gratified." Ptolemy: Bosch, with his customary stated uncertainty. Sable: Wren. Two votes Ptolemy, one Bosch, one Wren.

**The reveal.** The wolf was **Sable**. Not one vote touched her. The blunt one, the impatient one, the one contemptuous of deliberation — she won by weaponizing exactly that reputation, hiding a wolf's lane-changes inside a hothead's temper. Ptolemy, the match's only innocent conviction target, went down for the crime of asking good questions. His scouting report said it plainly: *reads as evasive — gets eliminated for looking suspicious even when innocent.* Called shot, made shot.

**Play of the match:** Sable's round-three "stop asking questions and vote" — the wolf ordering the village to hurry up and hang the wrong man.
